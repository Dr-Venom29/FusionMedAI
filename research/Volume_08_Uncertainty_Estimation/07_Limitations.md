# 7. Limitations and Future Work

This chapter discusses the limitations of the uncertainty estimation pipeline implemented in Step 8 and suggests directions for future research.

---

## 7.1 Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Validation Limitations

A core promise of uncertainty estimation (particularly epistemic uncertainty via Mutual Information) is the ability to identify out-of-distribution (OOD) images. OOD samples in retinal screening could include:
- Non-retinal images (accidental uploads).
- Retinal scans of different imaging modalities (e.g., optical coherence tomography).
- Scans containing pathologies not represented in the training set (e.g., age-related macular degeneration, glaucoma, retinitis pigmentosa).
- Extremely blurred or overexposed scans.

### 7.1.1 Current Limitation
In this evaluation, we implemented the mathematical framework and infrastructure for OOD detection. However, **no formal OOD validation was conducted** due to the absence of a verified, independent external OOD dataset in the frozen workspace. 

### 7.1.2 Scientific Position
We explicitly do not claim OOD detection capability based solely on the in-distribution APTOS test set. Claiming OOD performance without testing on true OOD samples is scientifically unsound. External OOD validation remains dedicated future work.

## 7.2 Computational Latency and Inference Costs

While MC Dropout provides richer uncertainty representations, its main drawback is computational expense.

### 7.2.1 Trade-off Analysis
- **Inference Latency**: For $N = 25$ passes, inference requires 25 forward passes per sample. On CPU-only hardware (such as our host environment), deterministic inference takes $\approx 0.15$ seconds per image, whereas MC Dropout takes $\approx 3.75$ seconds per image.
- **Hardware Footprint**: In resource-constrained clinical settings (e.g., portable fundus cameras, local clinic edge servers), a 25-fold latency penalty is often prohibitive for real-time screening.
- **Energy and Carbon Footprint**: Scaled to millions of screening images annually, multi-pass inference consumes significantly more electricity compared to single-pass calibrated networks.

## 7.3 Structural Constraints of Dropout Placement

In our model inspect, we discovered that the pre-trained EfficientNet-B3 baseline contains exactly one dropout layer:

`backbone.classifier.0` ($p=0.3$)

This layer is located at the very end of the model, right before the linear head. 

### 7.3.1 Implication
- **Shallow Stochasticity**: Enabling dropout at `backbone.classifier.0` only introduces stochasticity in the final linear projection. It does *not* perturb the feature extractor (the convolutional backbone).
- **Representation Congruence**: As a result, the feature maps generated by the convolutional blocks remain completely identical across all $N$ passes. The stochastic variation only reflects projection confusion.
- **Alternative**: Applying stochastic depth or inserting dropout layers within the convolutional blocks (e.g., in the inverted residual blocks) would perturb the representation extraction process itself. However, introducing dropout into a pre-trained backbone that was trained *without* it would degrade model performance. We chose to keep the model weights frozen and rely on the classification head's dropout, acknowledging that this represents a restricted parameter perturbation.
