# Document 01: Calibration Protocol & Invariants

## 1. Scientific Context & Purpose
In clinical decision-support systems, binary classification metrics such as ROC-AUC or PR-AUC measure discrimination—the model's capacity to rank higher-risk encounters above lower-risk encounters. However, clinical actionability requires **calibration**: ensuring that a predicted probability $\hat{p} = 0.15$ corresponds to an observed empirical event rate of approximately $15\%$ among patients with identical risk attributions.

Phase C7 investigates the probability calibration and statistical reliability of the frozen tabular candidate model identified in Phase C5 and audited in Phase C6.

---

## 2. Experimental Invariants & Constraints

To maintain strict scientific integrity and prevent methodological artifacts, Phase C7 adheres to four non-negotiable invariants:

### Invariant 1: Strictly Frozen Model Backbone
The underlying CatBoost architecture, decision tree parameters, feature transformations, and random seed are strictly locked:
- **Model**: CatBoost HPO Candidate (Trial 2 configuration)
- **Hyperparameters**: `depth = 4`, `learning_rate = 0.1383`, `iterations = 350`, `l2_leaf_reg = 2.911`, `subsample = 0.655`, `random_seed = 42`
- **Feature Space**: $D = 119$ clinical features generated by `ClinicalPreprocessor(scale_numerical=True)`
- **Data Partitions**:
  - Training Set: $N_{\text{train}} = 69,519$ encounters ($48,993$ unique patients)
  - Validation Set: $N_{\text{val}} = 14,911$ encounters ($10,498$ unique patients)
  - Test Set: $N_{\text{test}} = 14,913$ encounters ($10,499$ unique patients)
- **Zero Retraining**: No tree splitting rules, leaf values, or gradient steps are altered in Phase C7.

### Invariant 2: Zero Test-Label Snooping
Calibration parameters—such as logistic intercept/slope in Platt scaling, piecewise bin thresholds in Isotonic regression, and shape parameters in Beta calibration—are fitted **strictly and exclusively on the validation set** $(X_{\text{val}}, y_{\text{val}})$. The locked test set $(X_{\text{test}}, y_{\text{test}})$ is accessed solely for out-of-sample inference and metric computation.

### Invariant 3: Preservation of Discriminative Structure
Post-hoc monotonic calibration mappings $g: [0, 1] \to [0, 1]$ preserve rank order. For any two patient encounters $i$ and $j$ where $p_i > p_j$, a strictly monotonic calibrator guarantees $g(p_i) > g(p_j)$. Consequently:
$$\text{ROC-AUC}(g(\hat{p})) = \text{ROC-AUC}(\hat{p})$$
$$\text{PR-AUC}(g(\hat{p})) = \text{PR-AUC}(\hat{p})$$
Non-parametric step functions (such as Isotonic Regression) may collapse ties across bins, slightly reducing continuous ranking precision on test data, which is explicitly quantified in this volume.

### Invariant 4: Non-Causal Probability Scope
Calibrated probabilities are intended to approximate the conditional event probability under the observed validation/test population and data-generating conditions:
$$\hat{p} \approx \mathbb{P}_{\text{cohort}}(Y = 1 \mid X = x)$$
They do **not** represent:
- Deterministic biological certainties for individual patients.
- Causal counterfactual probabilities under clinical interventions (e.g., probability of readmission if care is altered).
- True ground-truth conditional probability functions outside the retrospective EHR cohort.

## 3. Methodological Scope & Limitations

### 3.1 Patient/Encounter Clustering Limitation
Although the train/validation/test partitions are strictly patient-grouped (preventing patient leakage across folds), calibration metrics remain retrospective estimates from this specific patient/encounter population. Repeated encounters within patients in longitudinal history mean ordinary independent and identically distributed (IID) assumptions can be imperfect; variance estimation should account for patient-level clustering where applicable (to be evaluated in Phase C8).

### 3.2 Internal vs. External Validation Scope
The reported calibration performance represents internal out-of-sample evaluation within the held-out cohort and does not establish external validity across hospitals, geographic populations, coding systems, or contemporary clinical practice. External transportability requires local recalibration of intercept and slope parameters.

---

## 4. Method Selection Criteria

Calibrator candidate selection is governed by a formal objective protocol:
1. **Primary Validation Selection Criterion**: Minimum Out-of-Fold Validation Log Loss (Negative Log-Likelihood, $\text{NLL}$).
2. **Secondary Criterion**: Expected Calibration Error ($\text{ECE}$) and Calibration Slope ($\beta_1 \approx 1.0$) on validation data.
3. **Generalization Safety Check**: Evaluating test slope decay and PR-AUC preservation to detect overfitting to validation bin structures.
4. **Final Deployment Contract**: No method is declared permanently frozen for production solely from this phase; final deployment selection will combine calibration quality with Phase C8 predictive uncertainty metrics.
